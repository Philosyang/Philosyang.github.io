---
title: "How to (not) Improve on Rhythm Games"
date: 2026-08-17
tags: ["rhythm-games"]
---

Every once in a while I see someone in some rhythm game server asking for advice on "how to improve on rhythm games."

First of all we agree on one thing to begin with — we play games for fun. The reason one wants to improve on the game is because we think that improving on the game can make it more fun. This makes sense, because, e.g., a higher skill level can unlock you higher difficulty charts that has fun patterns, or things like accomplishing "I can't do this chart at all back then, but now I'm dancing with it" makes you feel good.

I put this upfront first because many games contain elements that can silently steer the game into a job. For example, map completion rewards, missions, etc. Note that I said "steer" but not "transform", because we are indeed happy when we get something whether it's an all perfect, a specific character/title/song that we wanted, or even physical prizes (hi ongeki pretty glittering paper). I have a mental check on "if the reward is gone, will I keep on doing what I'm doing" — yes means I'm having fun in the process, hesitation means I'm not having as much fun in hope of getting something fun in the end. The latter is not wrong. Think of this like the famous delayed gratification experiment except with a twist — "choice A is to have a constant supply of marshmallows forever, while choice B is to have no marshmallows now, but you will be able to get an amount of marshmallows in a future point-in-time." If choice A is 1 marshmallow per minute and choice B is to have 100 marshmallows an hour later, it's fair to opt B. If the prize enables you more fun for quite a while, it is meaningful to delay gratification. Gauge the extra marshmallow amount as well as the time/effort it takes you _before_ you make the choice, otherwise you are taking on extra enemies like sunk cost, loss aversion, and many more which can lock yourself into the less fun route further.

We should also agree that fun sits between overly boring and overly challenging. It's a per-person sweet spot. Hitting middle C 4/4 for a minute can be boring. Playing a chart/score that you cannot even properly read can be overly challenging.

Therefore, the answer to "how to improve on rhythm games" might just be by "not seeking to improve". By keeping at "playing it for fun" mode, you automatically learn new things gradually and in the end you will just improve without you doing anything less fun. Furthermore, if I don't feel fun playing the game today, skip and look for other fun things; if I really want to (do you really want to? refer to the marshmallow scale) grind something out, go for it after you measure the rewards.
